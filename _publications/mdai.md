Deep Reinforcement Learning is used in many complex control
problems, such as autonomous driving and control of humanoid
robots, but the often complex design of a reward function remains one of
its key challenges. Goal-oriented specification addresses this problem and
offers an alternative approach to classical reward design. However, it is
limited to intuitive and low-dimensional state spaces. This work extends
goal-oriented specification by enabling training from high-dimensional
state spaces while preserving the intuitive feature space used for goal
specification. Using four diverse case studies, we demonstrate that we
are not only able to achieve high success rates, but we can also remove
unnecessary feature extraction from the deployment of the trained agent.
With this, we overcome prior limitations of goal-oriented specification
and enable its application to high-dimensional state spaces.
